# Assessing the Impact of Personality Traits on Telehealth vs. In-Person Therapy Preferences

## Executive Summary
This repository contains the capstone research paper investigating how introversion and extroversion personality traits influence individual attitudes toward online therapy versus traditional face-to-face counseling. 

Utilizing standardized psychometric scales and a $2 \times 2$ mixed-design ANOVA, this study evaluated differences in perceived **discomfort** and **value** across modalities among university students ($N = 46$; final analytical sample $N = 35$).

---

## Key Findings

* **The Introvert Discomfort Trend:** Contrary to expectations, introverts reported higher discomfort across *both* online ($M = 14.10$) and face-to-face ($M = 11.80$) therapy modalities compared to extroverts ($M = 11.07$ and $M = 10.00$, respectively; $p = .022$).
* **Preference for In-Person Value:** Regardless of personality type, participants perceived face-to-face therapy as significantly higher in value ($M = 21.17$) than online therapy ($M = 18.83$; $p = .001$).
* **Virtual Dynamics & Camera Fatigue:** Rather than offering a low-pressure alternative, online video sessions may introduce unique social pressures for introverts, such as self-monitoring and camera-induced self-awareness, resulting in heightened discomfort.

---

## Methodology & Measures

* **Study Design:** Quantitative survey study using a $2 \times 2$ Mixed-Design ANOVA (Therapy Type $\times$ Personality Type).
* **Personality Assessment:** Big Five Inventory (Goldberg, 1992) — Extroversion/Introversion subscale.
* **Attitude & Preference Measures:**
  * Face-to-Face Counseling Attitude Scale (FCAS; Rochlen et al., 2004)
  * Online Counseling Attitudes Scale (OCAS; Rochlen et al., 2004)
* **Sample & Workflow:**
  * Total survey respondents: $N = 46$
  * Analytical sample ($N = 35$): 11 participants scoring in the neutral range on the extroversion continuum were excluded to facilitate comparative group analysis between introverted ($N = 20$) and extroverted ($N = 15$) cohorts.
